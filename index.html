About the Film

In the quiet hills of rural Jamaica, some truths are too heavy to speak aloud. Bitter Blood uncovers a dark, generational taboo — the secret lives, second families, and unspoken infidelities that split households and destroy lineages.

When Enid leaves her toddler in the hands of her husband's mistress to work the fields, a desperate compromise turns into an unthinkable tragedy. By the time the sun sets, a child is dead in the river, and a family is bound forever by silence, suspicion, and a devastating question that remains unanswered to this day: was it a horrific accident, or an act of cold malice?

Director's Statement
“

Bitter Blood is my way of honoring my grandmother's pain and giving voice to a story that was never allowed to be spoken.

Growing up, my grandmother was always a complex woman — formidable, quiet, and guarded in ways I never fully understood. For over three decades of my life, the full truth of her story remained hidden in the shadows of our family's past, kept quiet by a culture that often buries its deepest wounds.

During the Christmas holidays of 2025, while on a drive with my family, my Aunt Jennifer — Auntie Jen, as we call her — shared a story that stopped me in my tracks. Auntie Jen has been the ultimate encyclopedia and godsend for my archival work on our family, especially when it comes to my grandmother and her 13 children. She has always been so descriptive, transparent, honest, and giving in how she shares our history. That day, she revealed the tragic loss of my grandmother's toddler son, left in the care of her husband's mistress while she worked the farm, only to be found face-down in the nearby river hours later. Hearing it broke my heart completely. The sheer weight of that loss, compounded by betrayal and unanswered questions, moved me to tears. Without Auntie Jen's openness, I could never have honored the true lived experience of this story.

In that moment, the visuals flooded my mind. I saw the quiet trails, the heat of the fields, the devastating silence of the river, and the undeniable humanity of a woman carrying an unbearable grief. I knew instantly that I had to make this film.

Bitter Blood is my way of honoring my grandmother's pain and giving voice to a story that was never allowed to be spoken. While rooted in a dark, taboo aspect of Jamaican family history, it speaks to universal themes of survival, betrayal, and the heavy secrets families carry across generations. I made this film to give her story a permanent space — to immortalize her memory and bring what was hidden in the dark into the light.

— Danielle Terrelonge
Director, Bitter Blood
Behind the Film

The Making of Bitter Blood

A Silent Canvas

In a departure from traditional modern Jamaican storytelling — where spoken dialogue and script drive the narrative — Bitter Blood operates as a semi-silent film. There is no verbal script and no spoken dialogue throughout the entire project.

Instead, the storyline relies purely on cinematic language: strategic POV angles, tight framing, key visual cues, and heavily amplified environmental audio. Aside from the natural rustle of the bushes and the flowing river, the silence is unbroken until the final sequence — punctuated only by the terrifying sounds of water and Enid's tragic scream upon discovery.

Four Hours in the Hills

Bitter Blood is a testament to fast-paced guerrilla filmmaking driven by deep passion and absolute trust. The entire project was captured across just two days, totaling roughly four hours of active camera time.

Originally, the vision was to shoot on location in Portland along the exact riverbanks where the historical tragedy unfolded. Tight scheduling forced a creative pivot — the director's own neighborhood, alongside a river, provided the authentic backdrop needed. Without a toddler actor available, the child's presence is built through implication, physical hand-offs, and subjective first-person camera perspectives — heightening the psychological suspense of the river sequence.
